## Research interests

I am interested in critical approaches to development, particularly through political ecology, post-development, and decolonial perspectives. Drawing on these perspectives, I explore how dominant ideas of development shape policies and practices, how they interact with local contexts, and what alternative forms of development might look like. Central to my interests are questions concerning the relationship between environmental and social transformations, people's attachment to place, patterns of mobility, and the power relations that shape their ability to influence decisions affecting their lives. A central question remains: What does development mean, who gets to define it, and whose visions of development are taken into account?

To approach these questions, I am interested in combining qualitative and spatial approaches to examine development processes across different scales. In particular, I am particularly interested in participatory methods, critical cartography, and close engagement with local communities.
